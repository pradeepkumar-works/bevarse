Timing lab

Two measurements decide the app's build: how fast a key press becomes sound in your browser, and which renderer draws falling notes smoothly. Nothing here is app code. Run it on your laptop and your phone, then press Copy results and paste them into the thread.

2. Play the keys

Tap the keys, use the computer keys A W S E D F T G Y H U J K, or play a MIDI keyboard. Each press logs how long the browser took to hand us the event.

3. Tap along to calibrate

Press Start, then tap the big button (or the space bar) on every click you hear. 16 clicks at 90 BPM; the first 4 are a count-in and are not scored. The result is how late your taps land against the sound, which is the offset the app would subtract.

Falling notes: Canvas 2D or Skia

Both draw the same notes. Skia here is CanvasKit 0.39.1, the engine React Native Skia uses on web. Run each for 10 seconds; the run with the lower 95th percentile frame time and fewer slow frames wins. Tap the roll to turn on sound; some notes then play as they reach the keys.
